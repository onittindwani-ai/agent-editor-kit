# Hook writing

Read `brand/profile.md`. If it is missing or a section this skill needs (`## Audience`, `## Voice and tone`, `## Script preferences`, `## Claims policy`) is empty, stop and run `ae-setup-interview` for that section (ask the user, with options), do not guess.

## The one strong rule

A hook may be bold only if every word of it is true and sourced. Never invent a result, a number, a quote, a before and after, a reaction or a story to make an opening stronger. If the true version is weaker than the bold version, use the true version and make it sharper with specifics instead. A bold hook that fails the truth test is deleted, not softened at the last minute.

## What a hook is for

A hook has one job: make the right viewer stay for the next sentence. It does that by showing, in the first 3 seconds, a specific thing that matters to them and a gap they want closed. It does NOT explain the video, introduce you, greet, or deliver the answer. The body pays the hook off; the hook never pays itself off.

Time math. Spoken at about 3 words per second, 3 seconds is 8 to 10 words. A full hook is 8 to 16 words, ideal 10 to 14, and it should end by about 4 to 5 seconds. Put the sharpest part first. A first sentence that needs 7 words of setup before the interesting part has already lost the viewer.

## Anatomy: anchor, tension, gap

Every strong hook has three parts, often inside one sentence:

1. Anchor: a specific, recognisable thing (a task, a tool, a number, an event, a place). Specific beats vague. "A 50 pound flour order" beats "a business expense".
2. Tension: something unexpected, costly, contradictory or surprising about the anchor.
3. Gap: what the viewer does not yet know. The gap is the open loop the video closes later.

Example (placeholder): "My sourdough starter smelled like nail polish remover, and the fix took one change." Anchor: starter smell. Tension: nail polish. Gap: what change.

## Hook families

Pick one family per hook. Full templates, test lines and extra examples are in `references/hook-families.md`. Summary:

| Family | Shape | Use when | True version test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Result first | "[Concrete result], in [time or effort]." | You can show the result | Can you show it on screen in the video? |
| 2 Contradiction | "[Common belief] is wrong, because [specific reason]." | The belief is real and widespread, the correction is checkable | Does a primary source back the correction? |
| 3 Before and after | "On [day] it was [state]. By [day] it was [state]." | A real change with dates | Are both states real and dated? |
| 4 The specific problem | "If your [specific thing] keeps [specific failure], this is why." | The audience has that exact failure | Is the cause checked, not guessed? |
| 5 The event | "[Who] just [did what], and it changes [what for you]." | News the audience cares about | Is the event verified at a primary source today? |
| 6 Friend reaction | "Well, that was not supposed to happen." then the fact | Light tone, a surprising true fact | Is the surprise real, not manufactured? |
| 7 Number with a reason | "[N] [things] did [outcome], just because [reason]." | A sourced number with a plain reason | Does the number have a ledger row and the right unit? |
| 8 The mistake | "I [did X] for [time], and it was costing me [what]." | The user's real experience (ask for specifics) | Did the user actually do this? |
| 9 Demonstration first | Open on the thing working, then one line | Visual proof exists | Is the demo real and shown unfaked? |
| 10 Specific question | "Why does [specific odd thing] happen?" | The question is concrete and the answer is in the video | Would a stranger ask this question? |

Rules for choosing:
- The hook family must match the evidence you actually have. No before and after without real dates; no result first without a result.
- Avoid using the same family two videos in a row. Check the project folder for the last hooks used.
- For a news video use family 5 or 3. For a how to use 1, 4 or 9. For a story from the user's life use 8 or 3.

## Open on the event or result, never the introduction

Bad openings (never use): a greeting, "today I want to show you", "in this video", "so", "okay so", the user's name or channel, a definition, a disclaimer, a long context sentence, a generic question ("Do you want to grow your business?"), a hype word ("insane", "crazy", "game changer").

Start on the most compelling TRUE thing: the event, the number with its reason, the contradiction, or the result. If the user's profile says their natural first words are casual ("okay", "so"), allow it only when the topic follows within the same breath and the whole hook still ends by about 7 seconds. Ask the user which they prefer during the interview (`ae-setup-interview`).

## The truth gate for every hook

Before a hook is shown to the user, run these six checks and write the result in the ledger (`ae-claims-and-truth-ledger`):

1. Is every word literally true, or clearly labelled opinion?
2. Does the script pay it off by the end at the same level of specificity (not a weaker answer)?
3. Would a careful reader of the source say the same thing?
4. Is every number checked today, with the right unit and fair rounding? "Up to" stays "up to".
5. Does it imply a guarantee, an endorsement or a result you cannot give? If so, rewrite.
6. Does it borrow credibility (a famous name, a company, a study, a ranking, a quote)? Then it must be a verified public fact from a primary source, with exact wording, a date, a ledger row, and no implied personal link or endorsement.

One claim, one source: a hook carries one claim from one named source. Do not put two sources' numbers side by side in one breath, because viewers attach the second number to the first source. If an idea needs two sources, split them with a clear transition and name each.

## The hook never pays itself off

The hook raises the question. It must not also state the verdict, score or result that the body reveals. Example of the failure: "I tested both tools. It was almost a tie." Now there is nothing to wait for. Fix: "I tested both tools on the same job, and the cheaper one failed at step two." The gap (why, and what the other did) remains open.

## The second line

The line after the hook must continue the thought, not contradict it, and start the reason or the answer. Bad: hook "This tool can build your whole report" then "but it really can't". Good: hook "This tool can build your report in ten minutes. Whether you can use it depends on one thing." then the thing.

## Opening stack for long form (first 20 to 30 seconds)

A long form opening may stack several short intrigue lines back to back, as long as every line is true and every opened loop is paid off later. Keep a loop table in the script notes: line, the question it opens, the minute it closes. Rules: no invented prevalence ("most people do this" only when the statement describes something defensible and sourced, such as how the topic is being covered); no promise the video does not keep; no more than 3 loops open at once; the first payoff arrives before minute 2.

## Text on screen and first frame (plan only after script approval)

When the script is approved, the hook's on screen text and first frame are planned by the graphics and caption skills. Rule to pass on: the first frame already shows a sharp, relevant anchor (the real thing, a real number, the face mid sentence), never an empty or loading looking frame; the on screen hook text repeats the claim in 3 to 6 words and is never different from what is spoken.

## Tests (run every one, record pass or fail)

| Test | How | Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Stranger | Read the hook cold, as someone outside the topic | They know who it is for and what is at stake |
| T2 Truth | The six checks above | All six pass |
| T3 Swap | Could this hook open ANY video in the niche? | No, it names this video's specific thing |
| T4 Payoff | Find the line in the script that closes the gap | It exists and is as specific as the hook |
| T5 Length | Count words, divide by 3 | 8 to 16 words, under about 5 seconds |
| T6 Sound off | Imagine it as text only | Still makes sense, no pronunciation puzzle |
| T7 No self payoff | Does the hook state the answer? | It does not |
| T8 Tone | Does it fit the profile's voice? | Yes, and no banned hype words |
| T9 Second line | Does line 2 continue it? | Yes |

A hook that fails T2 is rejected. A hook that fails any other test is rewritten once, then the user is asked to choose between the two best.

## Producing options

For a real request give 6 hooks across at least 4 families, ranked, each with: family, the line, word count and seconds, the evidence it rests on, the line that pays it off, and a one line risk. Recommend one and say why. Do not generate 30 hooks; six tested beats thirty untested.

## A/B testing hooks

Keep the body identical and change only the first 3 seconds. Post on different days at the same time (not the same day on the same account). Compare the platform's own hook metrics (skip or swipe away rate, 3 second retention, average watch time) at the same age, same length and same topic family. One result is a hint, not a rule: repeat the format on another topic first. Record hook text, family and the numbers in the project notes. Outside benchmark numbers are dated folklore; the user's own recent videos set the baseline.

## Worked example (placeholder)

Brief: a home baker's video on why bread comes out dense. Evidence: the user's own tests plus a named public baking reference. Hooks:

1. Family 4 (specific problem): "If your loaf comes out dense, it is probably not your oven." 13 words, about 4.3 s. Evidence: user's test log. Payoff: line 5 names the real cause (underproofed dough). Risk: "probably" must stay honest.
2. Family 2 (contradiction): "Kneading longer will not fix dense bread, and here is the reason." Evidence: reference page, ledger row 2. Payoff: line 4.
3. Family 3 (before and after): "Last month my loaf was a brick. This week it rose to twice the height." Only valid if the user has dated photos and the measured heights. If not, discard.
4. Family 8 (the mistake): "I cut my proofing time for a year, and every loaf paid for it." Only if true for the user; ask.
5. Family 9 (demonstration): open on a sliced loaf, then "Same flour, same oven, one change." Needs the real footage.
6. Family 10 (specific question): "Why does a loaf collapse in the last 5 minutes of baking?" Evidence: reference page.

Recommended: 1 or 2. Rejected as written: 3 (no dated evidence yet). Rejected: any hook starting "Today I want to show you".

Before and after fix: weak "Do you struggle with dense bread?" (generic question, T3 fail) becomes "Dense bread usually means one thing went wrong before the oven." (specific, checkable, opens the gap).

## Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Hook states the verdict | Move the verdict to the payoff |
| Two numbers from two sources | Split with a transition, name each source |
| Fake urgency ("you need to see this now") | Delete, replace with the specific consequence |
| Borrowed fame implying endorsement | Remove, or cite the primary source only |
| Fits any video | Add the specific anchor from this video |
| Longer than 16 words | Cut setup, keep anchor and tension |
| Hook promises, body delivers less | Weaken the hook to what the body delivers |

## Checklist

- [ ] Profile read; voice and claims policy applied.
- [ ] One family chosen per option, evidence exists for it.
- [ ] Every hook passed the six truth checks and nine tests.
- [ ] 8 to 16 words, sharpest part first, no greeting or introduction.
- [ ] Hook does not state its own payoff; second line continues it.
- [ ] Ledger rows added for every number, name and comparison in the hook.
- [ ] Six options shown, ranked, with a recommendation; user chooses.
- [ ] Nothing planned for visuals until the script is approved.
