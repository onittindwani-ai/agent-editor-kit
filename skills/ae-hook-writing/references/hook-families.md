# Hook families: templates, true version tests and worked examples

All subjects are invented placeholders. Replace brackets with verified facts from the claim ledger. If you cannot fill a bracket truthfully, drop the family.

## Family 1: Result first
Template: "[Concrete result] in [time, cost or effort]."
Use: you can show the result on screen within 5 seconds.
True version test: the result happened, is shown unedited, and the time or cost is measured.
Weak: "You can get amazing results with this tool."
Strong (placeholder): "A one page feeding schedule fixed my starter in 3 days." Needs: the user's real log.
Bad variant (never): inventing the number of days.

## Family 2: Contradiction
Template: "[Widely held belief] is wrong, because [specific reason]." or "[Belief]. [Short true correction]."
Use: the belief is genuinely common and you can cite a primary source for the correction.
True version test: name the source that contradicts the belief; is it still current today?
Weak: "Everything you know about bread is a lie."
Strong: "More kneading does not fix dense bread, and one baking reference explains why."
Risk: attacking a person or company. Contradict the idea, never the person.

## Family 3: Before and after
Template: "On [day] [state A]. By [day] [state B]."
Use: a real change with dates.
True version test: both states are documented and dated; the time between is real.
Weak: "I went from nothing to success."
Strong: "Last month this loaf was a brick. This week it doubled in height." Needs dated photos and measurements.
Note: for events older than about 30 days, say when relative to today and put the full date in the handoff.

## Family 4: The specific problem
Template: "If your [specific thing] keeps [specific failure], [the cause is not what you think / here is the cause]."
Use: the audience has that exact failure and you verified the cause.
True version test: the cause is checked (own test or primary source), not guessed.
Weak: "Struggling with your business?"
Strong: "If your loaf comes out dense, it is probably not your oven."
Keep "probably" or "usually" when the evidence is only usually true.

## Family 5: The event
Template: "[Who] just [did what], and it changes [what, for the viewer]."
Use: news the audience cares about.
True version test: verify at the primary source today; state the date; separate the event from your interpretation.
Weak: "Big news in the baking world."
Strong: "A flour supplier just changed its labelling, and it changes how you read protein content." (placeholder event, must be real)
Never claim to know why something happened unless a source says so.

## Family 6: Friend reaction
Template: "[Short natural reaction]. [True surprising fact]."
Use: light, friendly tone; the user's profile voice allows humour.
True version test: the fact is true; the surprise is real, not manufactured.
Weak: "You will not believe what happened."
Strong: "Well, that was not on the label. The bag says 12 percent protein, the lab sheet says 10." (placeholder; needs a real sheet)
Rule: the humour sits in the reaction and the story, never at the expense of a private person.

## Family 7: Number with a reason
Template: "[N] [things] [did outcome], just because [reason]."
Use: a sourced number with a plain reason attached.
True version test: ledger row with unit, source, date; the reason is the source's, not your guess.
Weak: "Huge numbers of people love this."
Strong: "[N] home bakers saved their starter with one change, just because they fed it twice a day." Only with a real survey or log.
Never show two sources' numbers in one breath.

## Family 8: The mistake
Template: "I [did X] for [time], and it [cost me what]."
Use: the user's own real experience; ask them for the exact detail.
True version test: the user confirms it happened; no invented duration or loss.
Weak: "I made a huge mistake once."
Strong: "I skipped the rest step for a year, and every loaf paid for it." (only if true)

## Family 9: Demonstration first
Template: open on the real thing working, one line over it: "[Same setup], [one change]."
Use: real footage or a real screen exists.
True version test: the demo is real, unfaked, and shows what the line says.
Never use a mock up as if it were real; label it if it is one.

## Family 10: Specific question
Template: "Why does [specific odd thing] happen?"
Use: the question is concrete and the video answers it.
True version test: a stranger would plausibly ask exactly this; the answer is in the script.
Weak: "Ever wondered about bread?"
Strong: "Why does a loaf collapse in the last 5 minutes of baking?"
A generic yes or no question is never a hook.

## Opening stack for long form (examples of line types, all must be true)
- "Imagine [specific true scenario]." then the real situation.
- "Here is the part the coverage skipped: [a true gap]." Only if you checked what the coverage left out.
- "Most of the coverage is about [A]. The part that changes your day is [B]." Allowed only when it describes the coverage you actually read.
Each line goes in the loop table: line, question opened, where it closes.

## Rewrite lab (before and after)
| Before | Problem | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Hey everyone, today we talk about pricing." | Greeting, no anchor | "Your price list is probably telling customers the wrong thing about your bread." |
| "This is the best tool ever." | Unsourced superlative | "This free tool scales a bread formula without breaking the ratios." |
| "Did you know that flour matters?" | Generic question | "Two flours with the same label can behave very differently in one loaf." (needs source) |
| "I will show you how I grew fast." | Vague, no proof | "I changed one line on my menu, and orders for that item doubled." (only if measured, with the real counts) |
| "You will not believe what AI can do." | Hype, no anchor | "An assistant wrote a full weekly order sheet from one photo of my shelf." (needs a real demo) |

## Quick selection guide
- No evidence yet: ask for evidence before writing; do not write the hook.
- Evidence is a number: family 7. A result: 1. A change over time: 3. A news event: 5. A personal lesson: 8. A visible demo: 9.
